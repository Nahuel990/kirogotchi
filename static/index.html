The quantum part

  • Qubit. The smallest thing a quantum computer stores. Why?

    A normal bit is 0 or 1. Why is a qubit different? It can hold a mix of both, written |0⟩ and |1⟩. What does it do? It holds the odds of each answer. How? The closer the qubit is to |1⟩, the more likely a measurement is to give 1.

  • Superposition. The mix itself: part |1⟩ and part |0⟩ at the same time. Why?

    Why is it not just an answer we don't know yet? Experiments show there is no hidden answer underneath. What does it do? It sets the odds: An equal superposition means a 50-in-100 chance of measuring 0 and a 50-in-100 chance of measuring 1. How? A gate called Hadamard can create this state.

  • Gate. The only thing a quantum computer can do to a qubit: turn it. Why?

    Why only turn? Reading it would be a measurement, and that ends the mix, so a gate works blind. What does it do? A gate changes the qubit's state in a precise way. It applies the same mathematical rule wherever the qubit starts. This turn is called Ry.

  • Measurement. Asking the qubit for an answer. Why?

    Why does asking change it? It can only answer 0 or 1, so asking forces it to pick. What does it do? It ends the mix: afterwards it really is |1⟩ or |0⟩. How? When measured, the qubit gives either 0 or 1. Its state determines the chances: 100% means always, 50% means half the time, and 0% means never.

  • Decoherence. The world bumping a qubit that nobody measures. Why?

    Why? Heat and stray signals reach even a sealed qubit. What does it do? It slowly erases the turns you made. How? Loses its delicate quantum properties through interactions with its surroundings.

  • Entanglement. Two qubits made into one mix. Why?

    Why one? A gate called CNOT flips the second qubit only when the first is |1⟩, so only both-|1⟩ or both-|0⟩ remain. What does it do? One measurement answers both. How? Nothing travels between them: there was only one mix to measure. Einstein called this "spooky action at a distance," and couldn't believe it; experiments proved it real.

How to play

  • Hatch your ghost (superposition) and name it. It starts half alive, half gone. That is real quantum physics.
  • Feed (gate) turns it a fixed amount. Play (gate) turns it a smaller one. The same press can raise the odds or lower them, depending on the moment.
  • It never stops turning, so the odds keep changing. Look when the alive ghost is brightest.
  • Look (measurement) is the big moment. The computer forces the ghost to be alive or gone. Alive, and it starts again fully alive. Gone, and it is gone for good.
  • Gone is final. No revives. Hatch a new ghost and try to beat your best.
  • The candle: drag it onto your ghost. Its heat turns it by an amount nobody knows. That is decoherence.
  • Bind (entanglement) with a friend's ghost code: you share one answer. The first to Look decides both ghosts at once. Einstein called it "spooky action at a distance."
  • Goal: survive as many Looks as you can. Your best is shown next to your ghost's name. Beat it.
